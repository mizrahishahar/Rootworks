# List building

The craft of deciding who a campaign reaches. A build arrives as a brief, who to reach and for what offer, and this skill owns every judgment from that brief to a segmented, send-ready pool: what makes one list, how big, from where, who on it is worth reaching, how it splits into campaigns, and which variables every row must carry.

This skill thinks in people and companies. Turning its verdicts into fields, formulas and views is table craft, a different expertise.

## The laws

- **The list is the lever.** When a campaign is dead, the list is the suspect roughly 80 percent of the time, not the copy. And the offer-to-list fit is decided here: the sharpest copy cannot save words sent to the wrong people.
- **The first list is a hypothesis.** The ICP gets refined in the inbox, not the spreadsheet. Ship the campaign you have and read the data rather than chase certainty before a single send.
- **One list has one centre.** One audience it describes specifically. If the describing sentence has to go generic to cover everyone, there are two lists.
- **Overshoot, never undershoot**, at every stage: the pull, the relevance cut, the segment boundary. Narrowing is cheap later; widening a pull that already landed is not.
- **Count before you conclude.** Every source has a free count before a pull; the base has an exact one after. Read the real distribution, never assume it. And count contacts, not companies: a 1,000-company TAM can be 5,000 to 10,000 contacts.
- **Variables ride with the pull.** A field the segments or the copy will need must arrive with the rows, not get bolted on after. Naming those variables happens at scoping, before anything is pulled.

## Ground yourself

Before scoping for a client: their registry row, their `overrides` KB row, what their past builds and campaigns already proved (the Hub carries every campaign's numbers), and, once rows exist, the real distribution in their base. Company data comes from DiscoLike; contacts come from the contact sources. Every paid pull is quoted and approved before it runs.
