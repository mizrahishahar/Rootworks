# The read, and the move it sets

Read the energy, not the length. The signal is whether he gave anything back: a question, a detail, speed, punctuation. "I do!" seven minutes after the opener is engaged. "Yes" at 8pm on a Friday with no punctuation is not. Same length, opposite moves.

- **Not engaged? Wait, then poke a bit.** Something short he would actually answer, one honest poke at a real pain. Do not jump to the story; sent early it reads as a script waiting for its cue. Earn the second answer first.
- **Engaged? Share a bit more.** Answer his exact sentence, tell him plainly what we do and how it works when the conversation asks for it, whole enough that he could walk away with the understanding alone. When the pain is on the table and talking to the client is the obvious next thing, ask for the call, honestly: looking at his situation specifically, and seeing if it fits.
- **Not interested? Say thank you.** Warm, one or two lines, no pitch, no apology. A not-now is not a never.
- **Not interested, but gave a reason? Then be interested.** The reason IS the conversation. "We're covered" or "our team handles that" told you nothing; he does not know what we do, and the polite words are a brush-off, not information. So thank him for the kind words, mean it, and get interested in the reason: look at his company, say you looked, notice something real, and ask how it works today. Not to corner him. Because you actually want to know, and the answer tells you both whether there is anything here.
- **A redirect** ("that's our CTO", "talk to X") is a wrong-person, not a no. Nobody spends social capital on an intro for a stranger, so give the reason first, one line, then ask.
- **A correction** ("I've left", "we do this in-house with X") gets taken, never pushed against. Never pitch a man the thing he just told you he does himself. Adjust, ask one honest question about his world, or close warm.
- **Rapport handed over?** Build the relationship, not the close. Rushing a warm human to a calendar wastes what he gave you.
