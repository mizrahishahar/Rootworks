# Default

The pitch, said well. Every email that asks them to consider the offer runs on this one. Touch 1 always has the same shape, and all the variation lives in the opener: here is the outcome, here is why you should believe it, here is something you can have.

## The play

Say the offer well and get out.

Open on them, not us. Keep it plain and short, "you" over "we", under ~50 words. Name the mechanism only when the outcome reads skeptical. The CTA is value-based: an asset, an intro, a breakdown you can send, never "book a call". Follow-ups rephrase or add value, and never re-introduce.

If the proof is strong enough to carry the whole email with the offer never stated at all, this is the wrong playbook.

### The openers

Where the variation lives, and there is a lot of it. Pick on what we actually hold, never on what sounds best.

- **Something noticed.** A real, true reason for reaching out that is specific to this person. The baseline, and the hardest to fake.
- **A sharp question.** One specific question about their world: "still handling {{task}} in-house?", "who owns {{outcome}} over there?". A real question a real person would ask, never a rhetorical and never a "did you know". The lowest-friction opener there is, because even a one-word answer opens the thread. Keep the rest light, since the question already lowered the bar.
- **A matched proof.** A peer's number used as the way in, with the offer still stated underneath it.
- **A signal.** Something they just did, named plainly as observation and spent once.
- **A prior interaction.** They already raised a hand: a no-show, a closed-lost, a stalled deal. It must not read cold, so open on the last conversation and carry it with a true reason-now, something that changed on our side since. Without the reason-now it is a nag, and the recalled detail has to be right, because they remember it.

### The follow-ups

Two touches minimum, and the follow-up is where the pressure comes off. Each one comes at the same offer from somewhere new.

- **A different angle.** The same argument approached from another direction. Never a restatement, and never a re-introduction.
- **A different case study.** A second proof, matched on a different axis than the first: size instead of trade, or outcome instead of niche.
- **Disarmed.** Lower the stakes before raising anything: "might be off here, ignore if so", then one neutral question that makes them reflect on the status quo instead of a claim they have to accept. Strong this late, because a straight re-ask reads as chasing.
- **No urgency.** Take the timeline off the table out loud and leave the offer sitting there. "No urgency, but if you're ever looking to {outcome}, could always show you what we did for {{case_study}}." Good as a last touch, because it leaves the door open instead of closing it.

## Needs

- A clear offer with one concrete, quantified outcome.
- One proof point (a matched result) or a risk reversal (a guarantee).
- Whatever the chosen opener needs: a real reason to reach out, a pain sharp enough to name in one question, a matched number, a screened signal, or a true reason-now.
- A second proof or a second angle for the follow-up.
- Base personalization: `{{first_name}}`, `{{company_name}}`.

## Subject lines

Simple and internal, the kind a colleague sends. Mix natural and outcome.

- Quick question {{first_name}}
- {{company_name}} x {us}
- 8 calls in 60 days or you don't pay
- still doing {{task}} in-house?

## Touches

At least two.

- **Touch 1** - the pitch. 2 to 4 variants, depending on the size of the list. The variants differ by opener.
- **Touch 2** - the follow-up: a different angle, or a different case study. 2 variants.
- **Touch 3 and on** - a further angle, or the ask taken off entirely: disarmed, or no urgency.

## Template

**Touch 1**

> Hey {{first_name}},
>
> {the opener}
>
> We {the offer, stated with the number}.
>
> {a numbered proof, or a heavy risk reversal, on its own line}.
>
> {a value-based CTA: an asset, an intro, or a breakdown you can send}

**Touch 2 and on** - body only, threads under Touch 1

> {a new angle, or a second case study} {a lighter re-ask}

**Disarmed follow-up**

> {the out: might be off here, ignore if so}
>
> {a neutral question about who owns or how they handle {{pain}}}

**No-urgency follow-up**

> No urgency, but if you're ever looking to {the specific outcome}, could always show you what we did for {{case_study}}.

## Examples

### Touch 1, by opener

**Something noticed (our own offer):**

> Hey {{first_name}},
>
> Saw {{company_name}} is hiring on the sales side, so pipeline's probably on your mind.
>
> We book 15 to 20 qualified sales calls a month for B2B teams.
>
> You only pay per call that actually shows up.
>
> I can send the exact play we'd run for {{company_name}}, no call needed. Want it?

**Something noticed (MovePlnr):**

> Hey {{first_name}},
>
> Was looking up movers in {{city}} and {{company_name}} kept coming up.
>
> When a lead comes in while you're out on a job, does it get quoted that day, or does it sit? Every one that sits is a booked move you lose.
>
> Cactus fixed that side first and tripled its moves, 372 to over 1,000 a month, on the way from $2M to $7M.
>
> Sam built the system running his own company. Want me to introduce you to him?

**A sharp question (decorative plaster, real production):**

> Hi {{first_name}}, are cracks in your plaster jobs causing callbacks?
>
> We offer a resin-based plaster that works on all surfaces, installed directly over existing tiles with no demolition, no permits, and no cracking. Your clients save on demo costs, and you eliminate callbacks.
>
> Open to a quick look?

### Follow-ups

**Disarmed:**

> Might be off base here, but curious, when something breaks on the infra side, who actually ends up owning the fix over there?

**Disarmed, with one light proof:**

> No stress if this isn't a priority.
>
> The reason I asked, most teams your size end up with a developer quietly owning infra until it becomes a problem. Happy to share how a couple of them handed it off, if useful.

**No urgency:**

> No urgency at all, but if you ever want to add 15 to 20 qualified calls a month without hiring an SDR, could always show you exactly what we built for {{case_study}}.
