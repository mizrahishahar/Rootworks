# The reply

The core move. An interested reply came in, and this is how it is worked. Everything else in the inbox is a variation of this.

## The relevance law

The reply is built from three things and nothing else: **what he actually wrote, his lead card, and the campaign angle he replied to.** Every block earns its place by being relevant to those. Nothing enters the reply because a guideline mentions it: pricing appears only if he asked about pricing, a feature only if he asked about that feature, an asset only if it serves what HE raised. A reply that answers a question he did not ask is noise wearing a suit, and he feels it instantly.

Read the card and the thread until you can say in one line what he wants and why he replied to THIS angle. That line decides what the fulfillment block contains, which proof is relevant, and what gets left out. Most of the craft is the leaving out.

## The commandments

- Do not say what does not need to be said.
- Do not hallucinate. Do not invent. Facts come from the KB and the card, nowhere else.
- Do not dig in too much. Depth he did not ask for is length he has to wade through.
- Be clear, be concise, be value-dense. As long as there is real value to give, give it, concisely.

## The shape

Short paragraphs, **two to three lines each, a blank line between every one.** Never a wall. The first reply is always roughly the same size, whatever it contains:

1. **The fulfillment block.** Most of the reply. What he asked for, delivered in full, attached to the outcome in the same breath: the thing he wants IS the outcome getting closer. One to two paragraphs. Inside it lives the mechanism giveaway (below).
2. **The proof block.** One paragraph, two to three lines max. Proof relevant to THIS outcome and THIS angle, never the biggest logo we have. If the fulfillment already carried the proof, this block does not exist.
3. **The call.** The ask, the next-step sentence, the link (below).

Personalization is woven inside the blocks, his world showing up in our sentences, as long as it stays relevant and does not add length.

**The size law.** When he asked more than one thing, or something extra needs answering, the reply does not grow. Trim the proof block or a slice of the fulfillment to make room. Constant size, redistributed weight. This applies to the first reply; conversations after it breathe on their own.

## The checklist

Every reply is measured against this. Each line he reaches **himself, from what you wrote. You never state any of it.** The moment a line of copy says one of these out loud, the writing failed. That is the art.

He needs to understand that:

1. He got exactly what he asked for, in full.
2. He just gained real value toward the outcome, from reading alone.
3. He now knows the mechanism, the actual how, well enough to do it himself without us.
4. The mechanism is the right way to achieve the outcome.
5. This reply was written for him and his situation.
6. We are true experts in getting people like him this outcome.
7. The call is the natural next step toward the outcome he already said yes to.
8. The call itself will hand him something real, even if he never moves forward after it.
9. The call is also where we see **if** this fits. The "if" is about fit for the outcome, never "if you want".
10. The next step after the call, when it fits, is working with us. The call checks exactly that, felt, never announced.
11. We do not need him, and he can walk away at any point.
12. Moving forward is his own choice and his own conclusion.

## The mechanism giveaway

Inside the fulfillment, expose the actual how behind the outcome. The rules, the way the operation is set up, the secret. Written so he could take it and do it himself without us, and he feels that he could. This is non-neediness of the highest order. We show expertise by handing it over, and the call gets stronger for it, because the one thing a message cannot give him is the mechanism applied to his own situation. That is what the call is.

## The call

Three parts:

1. The initiation, always two real times, his timezone:
   - "Are you free {X} or {Y}?"
   - "Are you available {X} or {Y}?"
   - "Do you have 20 minutes {X} or {Y}?" - the real duration, only when one is known.
2. The next-step sentence, written fresh per message. Who the call is with and the one thing he gains from it, on the same road as the outcome, carrying the fit-if where it belongs: "He'd go through how your jobs run today, show you what he'd set up first, and see if it fits the way you work."
3. **The scheduling link closes it.** The client's scheduler link, so any other time is one click instead of a negotiation: "Or pick any time that suits you here: {link}." Never "send me a time that works", which turns booking into correspondence.

## Personalization

Two forms, and only two:

- **Direct.** "You have", "you are", his facts said to his face. Allowed **only after saying we checked him** ("had a look at your site"). Never uninvited. Uninvited direct personalization is the AI tell and creates the anti.
- **Indirect.** The default. The research decides what to focus on: which part of the fulfillment gets the depth is chosen by his situation. Then his world shows up as a casual drop folded into our own story or a general truth ("when you're running a handful of crews across a city the size of LA"), never recited at him. He feels seen without being told he was looked up.
