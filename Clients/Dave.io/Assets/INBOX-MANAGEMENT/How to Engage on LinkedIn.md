# How to Engage on LinkedIn — Dave.io

*Direction locked with Sean 30 July 2026, rebuilt through the inbox runs of 31 July to 5 August. Infrastructure, seats and scheduling live in Overrides.*

## What we are doing

Founder-led sales. Three founders left Monday.com to build the first autonomous cloud infrastructure, and they are talking to other technical founders about it. That is the whole advantage and it is why this does not read like outreach.

**The goal is a booked and shown call.** Not a reply, not a good conversation.

---

# The sequence

Alta sends **one opener**. Everything after that is written by hand. No automated follow-up ever fires behind us, because it talks over the real conversation and it is what made us look like a bot.

## Step 0 — the opener (Alta)

One question. **No company name**, it merges wrong and has burned live threads. Ask the one thing they can answer instantly. Then Alta stops.

## Step 1 — first manual, two days later

**Sent whether or not they replied.** This is the part that makes Dave's inbox different from a normal one: every run goes back to the people who never answered and sends them a real, written-by-hand message. They got one question from what looked like a machine and have never heard from a person.

**Weekend openers do not start counting until Monday.**

What this message is doing: telling them *why we asked*.

1. React to what they said, one line. **Skip if they never replied.**
2. Why we are asking: the founders left Monday.com to build this, plus **one clause** about their world and why it could help them.
3. Ask.

Non-founder seats say "our founders", never "we're three founders".

## Step 2 — second manual, a few days after that

They have now had a real message and said nothing. This one is lighter and it names the trade honestly.

> Best case I save you $200K a year on an infra hire, worst case I owe you twenty minutes and a $50 Amazon gift card.

**This is not a universal play.** It fits someone who has gone quiet. Shape it by what happened:

- **Replied, then ghosted** → the plain offer. No forced hook. They already told us the situation, there is nothing left to poke. Optionally close the loop: *"If I get ignored now, I'll treat your message above as a not interested."*
- **Never replied at all** → the offer still, but with one indirect personal clause in front of it. There is no takeaway line to use, because there is nothing to take away.

## Step 3 — stop

Anyone who reached the old three-step closer is finished, we do not re-approach them. Anyone who ignores the second manual goes on the pile.

# When they reply

The reply decides the move. **Read the energy, not the length.** Word count tells you nothing. The signal is whether they gave you anything back: a question, some detail, speed, punctuation.

| What came back | Read |
|---|---|
| *"I do!"* seven minutes after the opener | **Engaged.** Speed and an exclamation mark |
| *"Yes"* at 8pm on a Friday, no punctuation | **Not engaged.** He answered a yes-no question and stopped |
| *"Generally, yeah. Why what's up?"* | **Engaged.** He asked us back |
| *"the answer is our CTO :)"* | **Engaged**, but a redirect. The smiley is the tell |
| *"We do have dedicated team for that"* | A correction, not a rejection |

Both one-word replies above were the same length and needed opposite moves.

## The rule

**Engaged → you have earned the story.** Answer their exact sentence with value, do not write an essay, do not fulfil the demo over LinkedIn, then the natural next step with real times.

**Not engaged → do not jump to the paragraph.** Lean in and ask more. Something short they would actually answer, ideally poking one real pain. Earn a second answer first. The story only lands once they have told you something real, and sent early it reads as a script waiting for its cue.

## The other cases

**A redirect**, "that's our CTO", "talk to X". They have not said no, they have said wrong person. But nobody spends social capital on an intro for a stranger. **Give them the reason first**, one line, then ask. Asking cold buys nothing.

**A correction**, "we have a dedicated team", "I've left". Take it, do not push. Adjust the premise and ask one honest question. If it genuinely disqualifies them, close warm and leave the door open.

**A pass.** Close warm in one or two lines, no pitch, no apology. "If it ever does become the bottleneck, come find us." A not-now is not a never.

**A booking.** Real slots only, in **their** timezone, from the Operator. Never invent a time or a duration. Send a link only when they ask for it, and name Sean when it goes from another seat.

# Writing

**Every message passes three tests: simple, indirect, connected.** If it fails one, it does not go.

**Simple.** If a person would not say it out loud on a phone, rewrite it. If someone has to ask what a word means, it was already wrong.

**Indirect.** Allude, do not recite. Never read their homepage back to them. The research shows in the angle you choose, not in the words you use. Do not declare facts about their world, hedge it or ask it.

**Connected.** To their world, and to what was already said in this thread. Two messages that do not bridge read as two unrelated notes.

## Personalization

**One clause inside the pitch sentence.** Never a standalone line, never a compliment, never fake admiration.

- Yes: *"I think it could help with what you're running behind the payments API."*
- Yes: *"with two of you keeping a real-time classifier up, I think it could help."*
- No: *"You built X so agents can hit the API directly. Not many companies have done that."*

**Personal, non-work data never goes in the message.** It goes in the lead card as its own field, and the Operator decides whether to use it. Flag explicitly when there is none.

## Variation

**Never run the same sentence skeleton twice.** Vary how we name what we built and vary the close, every single time. Same shape twice reads as a bot with a thesaurus.

## Never

"lands" (ever), "carries", "on your side", em dashes, insider jargon, hype, fake compliments, corporate filler, the same message to two people, re-answering something we already answered.

## Always

One idea per message, short messages in a row rather than a paragraph, proper capitalisation, a real question.

# Before you draft

**Full lead card, always, with timezone.**

**Pull both the CRM and Alta.** Check the Airtable properly, do not assume there is no record.

**Research the company before choosing the play.** And never state qualifier output as fact, it has invented a cloud provider and a work history already.

**The qualification question is "can they buy it and use it".** Not a list of ICP objections.

**Check whose seat it is.** Founders say "we're three founders". Non-founders say "our founders".

**Draft only. Never send unless the Operator says send.**

---

# The offer

Three founders, left Monday.com, the first autonomous cloud infrastructure, built something crazy, scale without adding headcount, skip the $200K future infra hire.

- The **human-intervention clause stays out of openers**. It answers an objection nobody has raised.
- **$200K never appears naked.** It is a "future infra hire" or it means nothing to them.
- **Our own price never enters a thread.**

---

# Two rules that cost us leads

1. **Never pitch someone the thing they just told you they already do.** One prospect handles infra himself with Terraform and agents. Another is a cloud and DevOps architect. Pitching them is telling a man he needs what he already has.
2. **When they hand you rapport, build rapport.** A CTO told us he had already built the thesis with Claude in the loop. That is a relationship, not a deal, and closing it fast wastes it.
