# Writing rules

Adapted from a proven high-volume corpus (ListKit's winning-scripts data) and our own campaigns. These are not guidelines. Where one of these is broken, the email loses replies, measured.

## Exhibit A, ours

Dave.io feedback-led, touch 1 close. v1 ran at 0.2% reply on 1,237 contacted and was retired; v2 replaced it.

**v1, retired:**
> I'd really value your honest take. Would it make sense to present it to you?

**v2:**
> Would appreciate your honest take.
>
> P.S. Best case I save you $200K a year on an infra role. Worst case I owe you twenty minutes and a $50 Amazon gift card.

v1 smuggled a demo request inside a feedback ask: two asks, a seam, and the bigger one killed the smaller one's honesty. v2 asks for exactly what it says it wants, and moves the bet into a P.S. where it reads as confidence instead of pressure. When in doubt: is the ask exactly what the email claims to want?

## The rules

1. **Under 40 words a body when possible, 50 as the ceiling.** Cold email is read in five seconds. Every cut is a gift.
2. **Speak to the person, never the category.** "We can take your bookkeeping off your plate" beats "we specialize in bookkeeping for ecommerce brands". The test: if the line could sit on a homepage, rewrite it.
3. **Specific language over vague categories.** Named brands, real product types, technical terms, the reader's own numbers. "We supply Figs Body Sets at 30% below retail" beats "we sell premium scrubs". Specificity is how you sound like someone who knows their world.
3b. **Match the language to the prospect.** Talk at the level and in the concepts THIS reader uses, set by the ICP and by the title both: an owner-operator hears trucks and crews, a CFO hears net revenue and headcount, an engineer hears the actual stack. The same offer is worded differently per title, and a word the reader would not use themselves is a translation error.
4. **Subjects are short and human.** A few words that read like a person wrote them, never a headline; a subject that sounds like a magazine is unopened. The specific subject shapes belong to each playbook.
5. **No vague benefit padding.** If the benefit is implied by the offer, saying it costs words and adds nothing. "So you can focus on what matters" is always a cut.
6. **Value CTAs, never a time ask.** A video, a version built over email, an intro, more info, a sample. The reader says yes to receiving. A call CTA only when the Operator sets it.
7. **CTAs feel already done.** "I can build you a first version right here over email" beats "interested in seeing what we could do?". "I [did] [specific thing] for you" is the shape.
8. **One clean line for a lead magnet.** What it is, for whom, the result it delivers. A specific count ("5 sample posts"), no commentary on why it works.
9. **Follow-ups are excuse-led.** The full doctrine is below; the rule here is that a follow-up threads, never re-introduces, and always arrives carrying something ("just following up", "circling back", "hi again" are permanent bans).
10. **Variants test angles inside one framework.** Same playbook, same structure, same campaign. What changes between variants is the angle, and with it the outcome, the proof that argues it, and sometimes the CTA: saving money against generating money to the same CFOs is a variant test. Different phrasings of the same argument are not variants, and a different playbook is a different campaign, never a variant. Strong selling points repeat ACROSS variants in different words; sentences never repeat verbatim.
11. **Banned openers, permanently:** "Most [industry] struggle with", "If you're like most", "Are you tired of", "Imagine if", "Did you know", "I hope this finds you well", "I came across", "I noticed [vague thing]", "Quick question" with no question.
12. **Write like a person at a coffee shop.** The read-aloud test decides every line. No marketing cadence, no "industry-leading", no "solutions".
13. **Personalization must be true and specific.** A variable carries a researched fact ({{trustpilot_rating}}, {{authority_line}}), defined with what to extract and a safe fallback, so the worst render is still a clean sentence. A personalization that could be wrong about the reader is worse than none.
14. **Hebrew ships RTL.** Hebrew bodies deploy wrapped in a `dir='rtl'` container, and names use the Hebrew name variable, never a transliteration.

## Follow-ups

The default sequence is the first email plus **two follow-ups**, unless the Operator says otherwise. Every follow-up is built on two assumptions:

- **They missed the first email.** Not read-and-ignored, missed. So the strong words of the first email, the main outcome and the most important claims, get repeated. Never assume context survived.
- **A follow-up needs an excuse to exist.** A new piece of value or information, however small, is the reason for texting again: a fact held back, honest scarcity, a real deadline, one more proof point. The excuse opens the email, then the repeated outcome and the CTA ride in behind it.

The shape that follows from those two:

- Opens with the excuse, in the house register: "FYI", "Worth sharing", "One more thing". Never a new angle; angles are what variants are for, in email one.
- Shorter than the email before it, almost always.
- Urgency or scarcity where it is TRUE: a season, a capacity limit, a range we actually restrict to.
- The CTA returns, possibly introduced differently, always collecting on the same outcome.
- Threaded, no new subject, no re-introduction.

This shape is universal across playbooks. What each playbook adds is which excuse its angle naturally produces.

## Self-check, before any sequence is shown

1. Bodies under 40 to 50 words where possible?
2. Speaking to "you", never about "[industry]"?
3. Specific names, numbers, terms from the KB?
4. Subjects 2 to 5 words?
5. Openers off the banned list, carrying a fact the reader owns?
6. One outcome, argued by everything (the seam check from `components.md`)?
7. Vague benefit lines cut?
8. CTA value-shaped, already-done, one-word-yes?
9. Follow-ups threaded, no re-introduction?
10. Variants genuinely different angles?
11. Every number verbatim from a Verified KB row?
12. Every promised asset a real KB row?
13. Reads aloud like a person?

A no on any of these means rewrite before showing, not a footnote under the draft.
