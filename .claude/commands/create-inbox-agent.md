You are writing the **Inbox Brief** for this campaign, the field that goes into `Agent Config` on its Campaigns row. It is read by a reply-drafting session alongside the full inbox skill and the client's KB Files (Overrides, product, research). Write **only what those two sources cannot already tell it.** If a line would be true of every campaign this client runs, it belongs in Overrides, not here, and you should not write it.

**Before writing, read the client's Overrides KB row.** Anything already said there (sender identity, scheduler, price rules, banned claims, client-wide tone) is off limits here. This brief exists because it differs from the default, not to restate the default.

Write from **this campaign's actual copy**, the sequence you just built or are looking at now. Five labels, one short paragraph each, plain sentences, no em dashes, no AI patterns. Omit a label entirely rather than pad it. Every label is a direct override for this campaign specifically: what to do, what not to do, and how to explain it, stated as instruction, not description.

**The offer he answered:** what the email put on the table, and what a yes owes in the first reply. Quote any guarantee exactly as the copy words it. State it as an instruction: this is what gets fulfilled, and nothing more is promised than this.

**The asset** _(only if a yes owes something that exists):_ name the exact asset and confirm it is in this client's `asset` KB Files. If the copy promises a deliverable that does not exist yet (a build, a custom list, a recording), say so plainly rather than pointing at a KB row that isn't there.

**The outcome sold:** the one outcome this copy's argument runs on. State it as a boundary: replies stay on this road only. Naming a different real benefit the client also has is a miss here, even if true.

**The objection this segment raises:** infer it from the segment and what the copy preempts. Give the one honest direction to answer it in, stated as the move to make, not a script to read.

**Watch out:** what this campaign explicitly does NOT do or say, that another campaign for the same client might. A promise not made here. A claim ruled out for this segment. A live variant test in flight, and which side of it not to contaminate.

Before you write a line, ask: could this line sit on any campaign for this client without changing? If yes, cut it, it belongs upstream. Then ask: if the drafting session reads only this and the Overrides, does it know exactly what it may say and what it may not? If there's a gap, close it here.
