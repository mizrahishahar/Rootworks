# Offer-led

The offer is the email. Not the main service, which is too large a thing to ask a stranger for, but a free first slice of the real work, spelled out so concretely that the only available reaction is why not. Nothing in the copy has to sell, because the offer already did.

## The play

State the free thing, spell it out, get out of the way.

- **The offer becomes the copy.** Write the offer properly and the email is already written; what is left is chunking it so it reads in a glance. If the copy has to work hard, the offer is not finished.
- **A deliverable, never a diagnosis.** Something they receive, not something you assess. An audit is a call in disguise, and a stranger has no standing to grade their business.
- **Spell out the work.** Naming the four things you would actually do beats naming the category. Visible effort on our side reads as effort removed from theirs.
- **Aim at why not.** They are running the value equation whether they know it or not: how badly they want the outcome, how likely it looks, how long it takes, what it costs them to find out. A free slice attacks the last three at once.
- **Eliminate risk casually.** A guarantee is a sales instrument and reads like one. Free until it works, or no hard feelings if it is not a fit, reads like a person.
- **Broadcast it, ration it.** The email goes to the whole segment; the free work goes only to the tier worth it. The criteria are the qualification, and saying them out loud is honest.
- **Ask for interest, not time.** They are agreeing to hear more, not to a meeting. That is what keeps the friction near zero while the thing on the table stays large.

## Needs

- A front-end offer that is a real deliverable, cheap enough to fulfil at volume and obviously worth money.
- The deliverable broken into its concrete parts, nameable in one line.
- Criteria deciding who actually receives it.
- Base personalization.

## Subject lines

Two to four words, lowercase, the way an internal note reads. No marketing language anywhere, and no pure mystery either: enough to know it concerns them, never enough to know a pitch is coming.

## Touches

Two, three at most.

- **Touch 1** - the free offer, spelled out. 2 to 4 variants.
- **Touch 2** - one lever, and the same ask softened. 2 variants.

## Template

**Touch 1**

> {{first_name}},
>
> {optional: one line naming the shift, or the pain they are living}
>
> If we could {the free deliverable, spelled out} {with only X of their time, or before any commitment}, {the ask for interest}
>
> {optional: whatever cannot afford to be skipped}

**Touch 2** - body only, threads under Touch 1, and reads cold on its own

> {one lever: the offer standing alone, or a different variable of the value equation, or what makes them eligible}
>
> {the same ask, softer}

## Examples

**Free creatives (paid social):**

> {{first_name}},
>
> Most brands are still bleeding budget on creative built for the old system.
>
> If we could ideate, design, script and edit five ad creatives for you completely free, with 10 to 15 minutes of your time, would that be worth sharing more?

**A list of their buyers (lead gen):**

> {{first_name}},
>
> If we could put together a list of the {{ICP}} in your market who are actively looking for {the thing they sell}, and hand it over whether or not we work together, would that be worth sharing more?

**Touch 2, the offer standing alone:**

> {{first_name}}, if we could build five ad creatives for you at no cost, would that be worth sharing more?

**Touch 2, the criteria:**

> Worth saying: we only do the free build for brands already spending, since that is where it actually proves anything. That is why you came up. Still happy to run it if useful.
