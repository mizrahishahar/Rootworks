# Asset-led

The email sells one thing: permission to send something. No pitch, no link, no calendar, because the asset behind it does all the selling. One line, one ask, and they either want the thing or they do not.

## The play

Sell the asset, not the offer.

- **One job.** Earn a yes to receiving something. Anything else in the email competes with that yes and lowers it.
- **One asset, built once, sent to everyone.** A recorded video per prospect does not scale and does not work better: a thousand leads is a thousand minutes before a single list is enriched. The trust comes from authority, not from hearing their own company name read back to them.
- **The asset carries what the email cannot.** The offer, the proof, the mechanism, and the way to book. That is exactly why the email gets to be one line.
- **Never send the link in the first email.** The yes is the point. Asking, then delivering, creates the small commitment that gets the asset actually watched.
- **No calendar link.** Booking lives inside the asset. A link and a calendar together turn a gift back into a pitch.
- **Write it like a text.** No greeting, no formality, one sentence. Formality signals a campaign.
- **Name a mechanism worth hearing about.** In a saturated category the asset is only interesting if what it explains is not already known. A generic walkthrough earns the yes and never gets watched.
- **Price it honestly.** This produces the most replies of any play and the least intent per reply. It is a volume instrument.

## Needs

- One asset, already built, that sells without you present.
- The offer, the proof and the booking path all living inside it.
- One credibility point, or one mechanism, sharp enough to carry a single line.
- Base personalization.

## Subject lines

Two to four words, lowercase, the way an internal note reads. No marketing language, and no pure mystery: enough to know it concerns them, never enough to know a pitch is coming.

## Touches

Two, three at most.

- **Touch 1** - what the asset is, and the ask to send it. 2 to 4 variants.
- **On reply** - the asset delivered, link alone.
- **Touch 2** - one lever, and the same ask softened, for those who never answered. 2 variants.

## Template

**Touch 1**

> {{first_name}}, {what the asset is} covering {the mechanism, or the credibility}.
>
> {the ask to send it}

**Touch 1, curiosity variant** - nothing offered, nothing explained, the reply is the only way to close the loop

> {the belief shift: at some point this stops being about X and starts being about Y}
>
> {the mirror: what most of them do not notice until it is too late}
>
> {what we are building, one line}
>
> {why this person specifically came up} {the ask to explain it}

**On reply**

> {the link}
>
> {one line: everything is in there}

**Touch 2** - body only, threads under Touch 1, and reads cold on its own

> {one lever: the outcome named plainly, or the asset made smaller, or what makes them eligible to receive it}
>
> {the same ask, softer}

## Examples

**Credibility-led:**

> {{first_name}}, I put together a video covering the growth framework I built to take {{peer}} from $5m to $15m in three years.
>
> Mind if I send it to you?

**Mechanism-led:**

> {{first_name}}, recorded a two-minute video explaining the system we built to find and book your buyers while they are complaining about competitors publicly.
>
> Do you mind if I send it?

**Curiosity variant:**

> {{first_name}},
>
> At a certain point what you're building stops being about {X} and starts being about {Y}.
>
> Most founders don't notice until it has already cost them.
>
> We're building {the thing}.
>
> Honestly, you came up because {a specific attribute} adds something we don't have yet. Happy to share the thinking if you're open.

**Touch 2, the outcome named:**

> {{first_name}}, worth saying plainly: it's how we {the outcome}, {at no cost until it works}.
>
> Still happy to send it over.
