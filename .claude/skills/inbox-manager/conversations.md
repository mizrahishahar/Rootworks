# Conversations

He wrote back with a question or an objection instead of taking the offer. The content can be anything, so no lookup table exists or should. The move is always the same, because underneath any question his value equation is running: how badly he wants the outcome, how likely it looks from here, how long it takes, and what it costs him to find out. Whatever he asked, he is asking whether this is worth it for him. Every answer lowers one of those variables honestly.

## The move

1. **Answer the actual sentence, honestly and fully.** Never dodge, never deflect to the call, never answer the abstract situation instead of what he asked. A dodged question is a trick. The answer alone should leave him knowing more.
2. **The answer carries the mechanism.** His question touches some part of the how, and explaining that part is the value. An objection is never argued. It is answered with how the thing actually works, and he re-concludes himself.
3. **What the email genuinely cannot answer becomes visible on its own.** His numbers, his setup, his process are not in front of us. Not withheld, just not here. That is what the call is, and it arrives naturally, through the same three call lines as the reply.
4. **Read the energy first.** An engaged question earns the full move. A flat one-worder has not, so ask one real question back and earn the next reply before any call talk.

## Price

The same rule with a client boundary. Honest at the level we can be honest: what is true in general (a range, the model, a free tier, no contract) gets said, and what depends on him is named as depending on him. Some clients keep numbers for the call entirely, and the overrides say so. Then we say that plainly instead of dancing. Never negotiate over email, never invent a number.

## The reference

"What does it cost?", where the client's pricing is public:

> There's a free plan up to 10 moves a month, then it starts at $100 and it's priced per location, no contract.
>
> Where you'd fall depends on volume, and honestly the number only means something next to what slow quotes cost you in a month. That's the math worth doing.
>
> Are you free Thursday at 11am or Friday at 2pm PT? Sam would run it with you on your own numbers and see if it pays for itself in your shop.
>
> If neither works, send me a time that suits you and I'll see if I can set it up.

Real numbers first, no dance. The dependency is honest. The call is where his math lives, and "see if it pays for itself" is the fit-if doing its work. A conversation closes with the own-time line, never the door out; the door out belongs to the chase.
