Teaches: what a cold traffic offer is, how to grade one before any copy is written, the latitude for framing it and the borders that never move.

# The offer

The reader does not know us, did not ask, and does not care. Zero trust, five seconds, real work waiting. Strangers respond to exactly one thing: a deal so obviously good that ignoring it feels like losing money. A service described well is not that. Most of what arrives calling itself an offer is a description with adjectives, and no copy fixes a description; naming that before launch costs a conversation, and discovering it after three thousand sends costs the list.

## Warm offer, cold reader

An offer is warm when the buyer arrives already wanting it: a referral, a search, a follower. Sales training, bookkeeping, a CRM: everyone knows these exist, and a person who wants one goes and gets one. Pushed cold, they fail whatever the words. Two ways out: reframe until it passes the grading below, or tighten the list until the reader is already in the situation the offer solves, which turns the list itself into the warmth. A parking lot contractor cannot cold-pitch paving; he can write to the twenty restaurants whose reviews complain about potholes.

## The grading

Five questions. An offer needs at least one strong answer; the more, the better. Three weak answers is not a cold offer.

| Question | Strong when |
|---|---|
| **What do they get?** | Money made, for this decision-maker. Money or time saved only when the saving is categorical, 80 to 90 percent, never ten. Reducing risk does not move a cold reader |
| **What is done for them?** | The work handled end to end, so they lift nothing. The line between premium and cheap |
| **What if it does not work?** | The risk sits on us: a guarantee with real conditions, pay per result, a free first slice built into the terms |
| **Have they seen this before?** | The mechanism is one they could not get from a search or a chatbot. Buyers are solution-aware; the known thing they would already have bought. A mechanism decays as it spreads, and works longest at the bottom of the market: watch it and be ready to replace it |
| **Does the proof transfer?** | A result at a company that looks like the reader: same trade, same size, same city. Proof they would not recognise adds nothing |

Strongest of all: free value embedded in the terms themselves, a free first slice, pay only per result. It lets the email's ask stay featherweight while the offer does the lifting.

## Framing, the full latitude

The offer usually reaches us as words we did not choose: a number, a guarantee, a deliverable. The job is making what we hold read as a no-brainer to a stranger, using language alone, inside fixed borders.

- **Surface the number.** The quantified gap is the strongest thing we have; put it where it cannot be missed.
- **Say the risk reversal in its sharpest true words.** "8 booked calls in 60 days or a full refund" beats "results or your money back".
- **Lead with the proof that fits this reader.** A roofing result sells roofers.
- **Reframe the category.** Bookkeeping is known; bookkeeping plus tax, opened with the study where one return sent to twenty firms came back with nineteen strategies, is a different offer with the same service behind it.
- **Compress.** Length is the enemy of a no-brainer.

## The borders

- **Nothing invented.** No client, number or case the knowledge base did not give.
- **The terms are not ours to change.** Scope, price, the guarantee's conditions, the deliverable. "Pay per call" does not become "pay per lead" because it reads better.
- **Nothing promised that cannot be delivered.** A named asset gets made; a number is real.

## The output

Before any playbook runs, the offer as it will be argued:

**The offer, sharpened.** One line, at its cold-facing best.

| Question | Rating | What we surface |
|---|---|---|
| What they get | Strong / Weak / Absent | the outcome for this decision-maker |
| Done for them | Strong / Weak / Absent | what is handled |
| If it fails | Strong / Weak / Absent | the guarantee, sharpest words |
| Seen before | Strong / Weak / Absent | the mechanism |
| Proof transfers | Strong / Weak / Absent | the closest result |

**Verdict.** Shippable as is; needs sharpening, with what; or not a cold offer, a flag, not a rewrite.
