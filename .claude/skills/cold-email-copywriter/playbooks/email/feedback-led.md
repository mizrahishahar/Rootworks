# Feedback-led

Not a pitch at all. You tell a respected operator what you built and ask for their blunt take. There is no commitment to refuse, so the guard never goes up. The exception among the playbooks: it needs no case study and no proof, because the build is the proof. It only runs when the thing you built is genuinely not a commodity.

## The play

Ask for the take, not the call.

- **Two lines is the whole email.** Line one is the offer stated as a build. Line two is the ask: their blunt take, if they are open to it. Nothing else goes in.
- **Say who built it, not only what was built.** An optional authority line in front of the build, about us: what we did before, what we came out of, what we already run. It costs a clause and it changes the read from a stranger's side project to something worth an opinion.
- **The build has to be real and uncommon.** A unique mechanism, a piece of software, a system new to their market. On a commodity offer this reads as a trick and dies.
- **Play to their position, not their posts.** The personalization slot is the authority line about them: why this person's read carries weight, specific to what they run or the space they operate in. Never "saw your post", never "noticed you're hiring".
- **Zero commitment anywhere.** No calendar, no terms, no guarantee. It reads like a prototype being shown around, because that is what it is.
- **Peer to peer.** Short, plain, the way you would text a colleague you respect.
- **Price the tradeoff.** Reply intent runs lower than a direct offer, because not everyone who answers is a buyer. Earning the first reply is the hard part, and the conversation is steerable once it is open.
- **Where the reply goes.** One of two ways. They take the call, and the 30 minutes turns into discovery and then the pitch. Or they ask you to send it, and you answer with an asset and work back toward the call. The call is always the end goal.

## Needs

- Something genuinely built and uncommon: a tool, a system, a mechanism the market has not seen.
- One dream outcome it produces, named in the prospect's language.
- A true reason this person's read carries weight: their position, their space, what they run.
- Optionally, a credential of our own that earns the right to have built it.
- An operator who can turn a feedback call into a discovery call.
- Base personalization.

## Subject lines

Small and personal, carrying no pitch at all. Two to four words, lowercase, the way an internal note reads.

## Touches

Two, three at most.

- **Touch 1** - the build and the feedback ask. 2 variants, with and without the authority line about us.
- **Touch 2** - excuse-led per the house doctrine: opens on the excuse ("FYI, I'm only asking..."), gives one lever (a concrete detail, who already runs it, or what makes them one of the few being asked), repeats the strongest line, then the same ask softened. 2 variants.

## Template

**Touch 1**

> {{first_name}}, {optional: who we are, the credential that earned the right to build it} we built {the thing, stated as a build}.
>
> {the authority line about them: why their read carries weight}
>
> {the ask for their take}

**Touch 2** - body only, threads under Touch 1, and reads cold on its own

> {one lever: a concrete detail about what it does, who it already works for, or what makes them one of the few people being asked}
>
> {the same ask, softer, or an offer to just send it over}

## Examples

**Lead-gen (our own offer):**

> {{first_name}}, we built a system that books qualified sales calls for B2B teams and only charges per call that actually shows up.
>
> You've been selling into this market longer than most, so your read would mean something here.
>
> Would appreciate your blunt take if you're open to it.

**With the authority line about us:**

> {{first_name}}, we're the team that ran outbound at {{peer}} before this, and we built a system that quotes an inbound lead the same hour it lands instead of it sitting until someone is free.
>
> You run one of the bigger fleets in {{city}}, so you'd know fast whether this is useful or nonsense.
>
> Open to giving me your honest take?

**Touch 2, a concrete detail:**

> Two teams are running it now and both stopped losing the after-hours leads entirely.
>
> Still curious what you'd make of it.

**Touch 2, softened to sending:**

> Easier if I just send it over and you tell me where it falls down?
