# How to Engage on LinkedIn — Dave.io

*Working file. Direction locked with Sean on 30 July 2026, refined through the inbox run of 31 July. Infrastructure, seats and scheduling live in Overrides.*

## What we are doing

Founder-led sales. Three founders left Monday.com to build the first autonomous cloud infrastructure, and they are talking to other technical founders about it. That is the whole advantage and it is why this does not read like outreach.

**The goal is a booked and shown call.** Not a reply, not a good conversation.

## The play

One question out. Then every reply is worked by hand. No automated follow-up ever fires behind us, because it talks over the real conversation and it is what made us look like a bot.

> ## Every inbox run works the silent ones too
>
> **This is the part that makes Dave's inbox different from a normal one.** A normal inbox run works replies. Here, every run also goes back to the people who **never answered** and sends them a real, written-by-hand message.
>
> They are not a lost list. They got one question from what looked like a machine, and they have never once heard from a person. The manual message is the first real contact they have had with us.
>
> **This happens on every run, without exception.** If a run only touched the repliers, the run is not finished.

## The core move

Three short messages, never one block.

1. **React to what they said.** One line, in their register. If there is nothing to react to because they never replied, skip it.
2. **Say why we are asking.** The founders left Monday.com to build this, and here is the one thing about their world that made us think it could help.
3. **Ask.**

From a non-founder seat it is "our founders left Monday.com," never "we're three founders."

**Say it differently every time.** Not always "the first autonomous cloud infrastructure," not always "we built something crazy." Same story, different words, or two prospects compare notes and we are a script.

## The second follow-up

If the manual message gets nothing, one more a few days later. Lighter, a little cheeky, with a spark of something about them.

> Hey, let me know what you think about talking. Best case I save you $200K a year, worst case I owe you twenty minutes and a $50 Amazon gift card.

Keep the number honest: a dedicated infra hire runs $200-250K a year before recruiting fees and the months it takes to ramp. Never put **our** price in a message.

## Personalization

One clause inside the pitch sentence, about what they actually do and why this could help them. Never a separate line, never a compliment.

- Yes: *"I think it could help with what you're running behind the payments API."*
- Yes: *"with two of you keeping a real-time classifier up, I think it could help."*
- No: *"You built X so agents can hit the API directly. Not many companies have done that."* Fake admiration reads as a script.

Read the person before drafting: title, whether they still work there, where they are. Roughly two thirds of the people we had been messaging were never buyers.

## The closes

Rotate them. Match the person.

- I'd like to hear your opinion on it, if that makes sense.
- I'd like to hear what you think, and see if we can help.
- If that sounds interesting, I'd love to have a chat.
- Can I show you what we built?
- **Hiring leads:** If you're open to hearing about it during your search, I'd like to talk.

## Voice

Simple, not clever. If a person would not say it out loud on a phone, rewrite it.

**Never:** "lands," "carries," "on your side," em dashes, insider jargon, hype, fake compliments, corporate filler, or the same message to two people.

**Always:** one idea per message, short messages in a row rather than a paragraph, proper capitalization, a real question.

## Two rules that cost us leads

1. **Never pitch someone the thing they just told you they already do.** One prospect handles infra himself with Terraform and agents, another is a cloud and DevOps architect. Pitching them is telling a man he needs what he already has.
2. **When they hand you rapport, build rapport.** A CTO told us he had already built the thesis with Claude in the loop. That is a relationship, not a deal, and closing it fast wastes it.
