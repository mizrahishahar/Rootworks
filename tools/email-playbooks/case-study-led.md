# Case-study-led

A peer's result, and nothing else. The offer is never stated: the proof replaces the pitch outright and the reader draws the conclusion themselves. Mover to mover, SaaS to SaaS. The tighter the match, the less you have to say.

## The play

Tell them what happened to someone like them, then ask if they want to know how.

- **Never state the offer.** No description of what we do, no claim about what it would do for them. The result is the entire argument. The moment you explain the offer, this becomes the default playbook with a proof sitting on top of it.
- **The peer carries it.** They believe it because the person it happened to is them: same trade, same size, same market. Match beats magnitude, and a modest number from a real peer outperforms a big number from a stranger.
- **Numbers plain, no adjectives.** "$2M to $7M on the same trucks." The figure does the work, and any word added to sell it weakens it.
- **Point at the lever, do not hand it over.** One line on what actually changed ("most of it came from fixing how the office handles leads, not the moves themselves"). Enough to make it feel real, not enough to make the ask unnecessary.
- **The ask is the artifact, never a call.** The breakdown, the write-up, an intro to the owner. They are saying yes to curiosity about a peer, not to a meeting.
- **The named owner is the strongest lever.** Naming the person and offering the intro turns curiosity into a relationship, and it is the split worth running against a no-name breakdown ask.
- **Follow-ups escalate the same story.** Not a new angle: the next number from the same case. One story deepening beats three stories competing.
- **No greeting where there is no name.** These land in role inboxes as often as personal ones, and opening straight on the result reads stronger than a greeting addressed to nobody.

## Needs

- One real case with real figures, matched to the segment by trade, size or market. No case, no playbook.
- The figures true and unrounded in our favour, and cleared for use.
- At least two further numbers from the same case, for the follow-ups.
- Ideally the owner's name, and their willingness to take an intro.
- Base personalization.

## Subject lines

The number itself, or a plain internal line. Never a claim.

- $2M to $7M on the same trucks
- {{result}} for a {{niche}} like yours
- question for {{company_name}}

## Touches

Two touches.

- **Touch 1** - the result and the ask for the artifact. 2 to 4 variants. The split worth running is a no-name breakdown ask against a named owner and an intro.
- **Touch 2** - the follow-up, the next number from the same case. 2 variants.

## Template

**Touch 1**

> {the peer and the result, in plain numbers}
>
> {one line on what actually changed}
>
> {the artifact offered: the breakdown, or an intro to the owner}

**Touch 2** - the follow-up, body only, threads under Touch 1

> {the next number from the same case} {the artifact offered again, lighter}

## Examples

Real production (MovePlnr, residential movers 1-10, company inboxes).

**Touch 1, no name, the breakdown**

> Cactus Moving grew from $2M to $7M without adding trucks first.
>
> Most of it came from fixing how the office handles leads, not the moves themselves.
>
> The owner wrote down what he changed. Want the breakdown?

**Touch 1, named owner, the intro**

> Sam grew Cactus Moving from $2M to $7M on the same trucks, then turned what he changed into a system other movers now run and grow on.
>
> He's offered to walk other owners through it. Want an intro?

**Touch 2, the next number**

> Turns out the same fix took Cactus from 372 moves a month to over 1,000.
>
> Happy to send over how they did it, if useful.

**Touch 2, scaled further, with the intro**

> Cactus is at 4 cities and 6,000 moves now, same playbook the whole way.
>
> Happy to intro you to the owner if it's worth a look.
