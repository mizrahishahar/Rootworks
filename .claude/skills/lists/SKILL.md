# Lists

The craft of deciding who a campaign reaches. A build arrives as a brief, who to reach and for what offer, and this skill owns every judgment from that brief to a segmented pool: how many lists, how big, who on them is worth reaching, how they split into campaigns, and which variables every row must carry. It thinks in people and companies; turning its verdicts into views is the clayroots skill, and the machines that land, find and verify are called through the automations skill.

The decisions that are not judgment (how many people per company, what deployable means, the fixed clauses of a segment) are the lists standard, handed to you. Read it; never restate it.

## The laws

- **The list is the lever.** When a campaign is dead, the list is the suspect most of the time, not the copy.
- **The first list is a hypothesis.** The ICP gets refined in the inbox, not the spreadsheet. Ship and read the data.
- **One list has one centre.** If the sentence describing it goes generic to cover everyone, there are two lists.
- **Overshoot, never undershoot**, at every stage: the pull, the relevance cut, the segment boundary. Narrowing is cheap later; widening a landed pull is not.
- **Count before you conclude.** Every source counts before a pull; the base counts exactly after. Count contacts, not companies.
- **Variables ride with the pull.** A field the segments or the copy will need is named at scoping and arrives with the rows.

## Ground yourself

Before scoping: the client's brief, how they differ from the default, what their past builds and campaigns already proved, and, once rows exist, the real distribution in their base. Every paid pull is quoted and approved before it runs.

## This folder

`scoping.md`: how many lists a brief warrants and how big. `relevance.md`: who is worth reaching, and the rule as a formula with its two views. `segmentation.md`: whether the pool splits, on what axis, in what priority.
