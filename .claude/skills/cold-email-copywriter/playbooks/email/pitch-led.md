# Pitch-led

## Description

The pitch, said well. Here is the outcome, here is why you should believe it, here is something you can have. Open on them, keep it plain, name the mechanism only when the outcome reads skeptical, close on a value CTA. All the variation lives in the opener: something noticed about them, a sharp question about their world, a matched proof, a signal they just gave, or a prior interaction carried with a true reason-now. If the proof is strong enough to carry the email with the offer never stated, this is the wrong playbook.

## Template

**Touch 1**

> Hey {{first_name}},
>
> {the opener}
>
> We {the offer, stated with the number}.
>
> {a numbered proof, or a heavy risk reversal, on its own line}
>
> {a value CTA: an asset, an intro, a breakdown you can send}

**Touch 2**, threaded

> {the reason to be here: a second proof, a held-back detail, honest scarcity}
>
> {the outcome, in fresh words} {the re-ask}

## Examples

**Something noticed**

> Hey {{first_name}},
>
> Saw {{company_name}} is hiring on the sales side, so pipeline's probably on your mind.
>
> We book 15 to 20 qualified sales calls a month for B2B teams.
>
> You only pay per call that actually shows up.
>
> I can send the exact play we'd run for {{company_name}}, no call needed. Want it?

**A sharp question**

> Hi {{first_name}}, are cracks in your plaster jobs causing callbacks?
>
> We offer a resin-based plaster that works on all surfaces, installed directly over existing tiles with no demolition, no permits, and no cracking. Your clients save on demo costs, and you eliminate callbacks.
>
> Open to a quick look?

**Touch 2, honest scarcity**

> FYI, we only offer this to brands in your Trustpilot range, because that is typically where the most net revenue gets lost.
>
> Same reason we guarantee it: half your inquiries resolved from day one, or you do not pay.
>
> I can build you the initial version now, over email. Want to see it?
